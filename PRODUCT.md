# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: visitors to berryde.com who open the projects section, such as recruiters, hiring managers and other developers. They usually arrive with no task in mind, spend a minute or two playing with it, and decide quickly whether it is impressive. The job is to make that first impression land and then reward a little exploration.

Route lookup ("where can I fly direct from here, and with whom?") is a side effect the data supports, not the audience being designed for.

## Product Purpose

An interactive visualisation of the world's passenger flight network: every airport with scheduled routes, where those routes go, which airlines fly them and how long they take. It is a v2 rewrite of a 2022 Svelte project and exists as a portfolio piece. Success means a visitor is impressed straight away, keeps clicking, and leaves thinking the author has real craft in data, interaction and frontend work.

## Positioning

The 2022 version drew 3,000 pre-filtered routes on a flat image of the world. v2 works with the whole network in the browser: about 89,000 airline-direction routes between about 3,900 airports, with airline and flight-time detail, delivered as a compact binary column file and needing no server. The difference from a generic route map is scale and detail rendered client-side, with exploration across airports, airlines and connections, not a single click-to-reveal view.

## Operating Context

- Reached from berryde.com/projects/flights, a separate Worker mounted on the personal site. Visitors come from the site, links or a CV.
- Sessions are short and exploratory, on desktop and on phones.
- The data is rebuilt by `npm run data` (Python + DuckDB in `pipeline/`), locally or in CI, and is not committed.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Planned capabilities for v2:
- Pick an airport and see all its routes.
- Search airports by city, name or IATA code.
- Airline view: pick an airline and see its whole network.
- A-to-B: pick two airports and see the direct routes or shortest connections between them.

Constraints:
- Static and free: a static-assets-only Cloudflare Worker. No Worker script, backend or API; everything is computed in the browser from `public/data/catalog.json` and `public/data/routes.bin` (see `wrangler.jsonc`).
- Must work well on phones: touch and small screens are first-class.
- Served under the base path `/projects/flights/`.
- Stack: Vite + React 19 + TypeScript + Tailwind v4.
- Airport and airline counts must fit Uint16 indices (enforced by the pipeline). Some routes have no named airline (`noAirline`).
- Terminology from the data: a *route* is one airline flying one way between two airports; *minutes* is the scheduled flight time.

Undecided:
- Shareable or deep-linkable URLs for selections.
- Stats and curiosities (longest routes, busiest hubs). Not requested for v2.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: "Flights".
- Standalone identity: it does not need to match berryde.com's look.
- The data source must be credited: Jonty/airline-route-data by Jonty Wareing, scraped from flightsfrom.com and published without a licence. It is used with credit for a non-commercial project.

## Evidence on Hand

- `public/data/catalog.json` + `routes.bin`, generated by the pipeline: airports (code, name, city, country, lat/lon, ranked busiest first), airlines (code, name), routes with flight times, and `downloadedAt` showing how current the data is.
- The old 2022 implementation and its screenshot on the `master` branch, for reference only.
- No testimonials, usage numbers, press or schedule/frequency data exist. Do not invent passenger volumes, flight frequencies, prices or live-flight data; the dataset has none of them.

## Product Principles

1. Impress in the first viewport: the network itself is the hero and should be engaging before anyone clicks.
2. Reward curiosity: every interaction reveals something real in the data, and getting from one exploration to the next takes one step.
3. Show the whole dataset honestly. Scale is the point, so don't fake it with sampling unless it's needed for performance, and don't make claims the data can't support.
4. Phone visitors get the full experience, not a reduced one.
5. Static and instant: all compute happens client-side, and load time and frame rate are part of the craft being shown off.
